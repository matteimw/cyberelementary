Our Story

Cyber Elementary started with one simple question from the dinner table — and a realization that the world's best cybersecurity training had never been written for kids.

The Story

It started at the dinner table.

My kids were eight and ten years old when I realized I had a problem. I'd spent three decades in cybersecurity — National Security Agency, USCENTCOM, enterprise managed security programs — and I could brief a Fortune 500 board on threat intelligence. But when my own children sat down at a screen, I didn't have a good answer for how to teach them to stay safe.

I looked for the kind of curriculum I'd want them to use. Something rigorous but age-appropriate. Lesson-plan ready. Written as if a real cybersecurity professional — not a children's textbook committee — had put it together. I couldn't find it.

So I wrote it myself.

"The best cybersecurity training in the world is worthless if it never reaches the people who need it most — and my kids needed it now." — Mark W. Mattei, LTC (USA, Ret.)

What started as a lesson plan for our dining room table grew into a full curriculum series — 36 video lessons, graded workbooks for 3rd through 6th grade, and teacher resources designed to drop straight into a classroom. Cyber Elementary is the program I wish had existed when my kids first picked up a device.

The Background

30 years of experience, distilled for kids.

A career in cyber gives you pattern recognition that's hard to put into words — you see the attacks, the failures, the human errors that real organizations pay billions trying to prevent. Most of those failures trace back to basic behaviors: clicking the wrong link, trusting the wrong message, sharing too much online.

Those basics are teachable. They're actually easier to learn early, before bad habits form. That insight — earned across a long career and a dinner table full of kids — is what every Cyber Elementary lesson is built on.

The books are published through Baldwin Terney Press, a family publishing house with roots going back three generations. The consulting practice behind the curriculum — Baldwin Terney Consulting — advises enterprise organizations on the same cybersecurity principles these lessons teach.
